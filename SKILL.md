# Article to Motion Video

Use this skill when the user wants an article turned into an explainable, editable HyperFrames video. Preserve the article's facts and links; compress only by selecting which stated points to show. Do not invent examples, statistics, voices, or source claims.

## Workflow

1. Read the article and define one audience, one takeaway, and a duration. Keep a compact storyboard in JSON before writing HTML.
2. Read [storyboard-schema.md](references/storyboard-schema.md), then create `storyboard.json` and `DESIGN.md` in the project.
3. Write `narration.txt` (blank-line-separated scene paragraphs) and `caption-plan.json`; see [caption-input.md](references/caption-input.md). Run `python scripts/generate_voice.py <project>` for Edge TTS narration and saved WordBoundary timing, then use the resulting segment durations and `narration-cues.json` to finalize the storyboard. Use `python scripts/align_captions.py <project>` to revise phrase breaks without another TTS request. Never estimate timestamps when real boundaries exist.
4. Run `scripts\run_pipeline.cmd <project> <storyboard.json>` on Windows to validate, generate `index.html`, and lint. Reuse these scripts; do not rewrite the generator per article. Set project-name beats from the matching spoken cue and preserve them while that point is explained. Use 3–4 short beats per scene in the fixed layout; split long material rather than crowding more rows. Supply scene `visualLabels` from the new article.
5. Run `npx.cmd hyperframes@0.8.31 check --snapshots`, inspect frames, then render with `npx.cmd hyperframes@0.8.31 render --quality high --output <file.mp4>` and verify with FFprobe. An existing request for a completed video authorizes rendering; ask only if the user requested storyboard-only review. Stop after two focused repair rounds or the same environment dependency fails twice. Assign one execution owner; do not launch duplicate renders. After checks pass, deliver instead of reopening design decisions.

## Visual contract

Pacing default: use a brisk, natural young Mandarin male narration. With Edge TTS `zh-CN-YunxiNeural`, start at `rate="+10%"` (the earlier test used `-20%`); keep the established pitch unless requested otherwise. Record the actual rate in `voice.rate`. For a different provider, choose its equivalent brisk delivery by listening rather than copying the percentage. Preview an excerpt containing Chinese text and English tool names; reduce to `+5%` if pronunciation or comprehension suffers.

Generate the faster narration first, then rebuild scene durations, beats, and subtitles from its actual timings. Do not speed only the visuals, reuse old subtitle timestamps, or force a 75-second runtime. Aim for a new point or meaningful visual change roughly every 2–3 seconds where the narration supports it; shorten idle holds, preserve breathing and reading time, and end about 0.4–0.8 seconds after the final spoken phrase. Keep the accepted smooth text entrances rather than accelerating every tween. Verify caption alignment and that each animated element settles before its clip ends. If retiming existing audio is explicitly requested, use pitch-preserving tempo adjustment and retime all dependent tracks together.

Personal brand default: place `杰瑞` as a persistent, small top-left background overlay throughout the video, including the closing content scene. At 1920x1080 use left 160px, top 40px, 32px type, above the scene layers and separate from the scene kicker. Do not add a large closing signature, a brand-only ending, or a spoken `杰瑞` sign-off. The final scene should conclude the article's takeaway; do not repeat the brand in its headline, accent, beats, or captions. If updating an older storyboard, remove its former closing brand text and corresponding narration before generating new audio.

The accepted dynamic treatment is the default for future articles: stagger titles and text upward with a visible 24–44px rise over roughly 0.45–0.8 seconds, reveal key points as the narration reaches them, animate cards/nodes separately, and draw connectors after their nodes. Retain independently timed narration captions with green keyword emphasis. Phrase-level captions are supported; do not claim word-level timing unless real word timestamps are available. Adapt beat count and timing to the new article and its actual audio rather than reusing the test article's 21 cues or 75-second duration.

The default composition is 1920x1080, 30fps, dark grid, pale foreground, and fluorescent yellow-green accent. Use the six scene types in the schema (`cover`, `comparison`, `flow`, `split`, `summary`, `outro`) when the request is a short explainer. Every addressable element needs a unique `id`, numeric `data-start`, `data-duration`, and `data-track-index`; the root needs `data-composition-id`. Register a paused GSAP timeline. Every scene needs at least three independent beats: text enters from below with finite seek-safe tweens, visual nodes/cards build in sequence, and connector lines draw after their nodes. Subtitles are independently timed `.clip` elements derived from the narration SRT, with highlighted keywords and a short fade at cue end. The dynamic acceptance pass must verify three visible actions per scene, no narration gap longer than about 1.5 seconds, subtitle coverage through the audio end, and snapshots showing intermediate states. Do not use `Math.random`, `Date.now`, infinite repeats, or display/visibility tweens. Lay out before animating, use transitions between scenes, and do not add exit animation to the final scene.

Read [dark-grid-style.md](references/dark-grid-style.md) for the palette, fonts, and motion limits. Read [windows-runtime.md](references/windows-runtime.md) for Windows command and failure handling. Deliver source project, storyboard, narration/voice status, MP4, contact sheet, and QA evidence when the environment allows; otherwise report the exact blocker and preserve the verifiable silent project.
